HOUSE CLEANING
You quote and book this one yourself, start to finish.
1. Verify the home
"I'm showing roughly _____ square feet. Does that sound about right?"
Google the address for square footage. Customers underestimate their own homes.
2. Discovery: find the real reason and what it is costing them
This is where the sale is made. If they have not already told you why they are calling:
"What made you decide to get the home cleaned right now?"
Now dig into the consequence. Let them feel the cost of not solving it. Pick one:
* "And if it stayed the way it is now, what would that look like when the guests arrive / when you're trying to rest and recover?"
* "How long has this been on your list that you keep meaning to get to?"
Let them answer fully. Do not fill the silence. Whatever they describe here is bigger than your price.


"What have you already tried?"
This surfaces the history of what has not worked, which is what makes them ready to pay for a real fix.
▸  If they used a cleaning company before:
"What did you like, and what would you want done differently this time?"
▸  If they tried doing it themselves:
"Why do you think that hasn't stuck?"


▸  If they are only asking for a partial cleaning (just the bathrooms, the floors, or the baseboards):
Do not lock into the small job. Someone who only wants those areas usually is not keeping up with the rest either. Give them a judgment-free way to say so:
"Makes sense on the bathrooms and floors. And the rest, the dusting, the cabinets, the baseboards, are you keeping up with that yourself, or is that the kind of thing that tends to pile up?"
'Tends to pile up' hands them an easy way to admit the gap. Never make it sound like they are letting the place go, that makes them defensive and they buy less. If they admit the rest is slipping, go straight to the widening trial close:
"If we could just keep the whole home clean for you, would that take a weight off your shoulders?"
This offers to close a gap they just described, not a new upsell. If they hold firm on the two rooms, book the two rooms and earn the rest later. Never push past a clear no.


"What's the one thing we could take care of that would make this a 5-star experience for you?"
Offer examples only if they stall: pet hair, soap buildup in the shower, grease in the oven. Write down their answer. You will use it later.
3. Present the value as questions, not a speech
"Sounds good. Let me put your estimate together. While I do that, can I ask you a couple of quick things so I know we're the right fit?"
Turn each of our strengths into a question tied to what they already told you. They will sell themselves.
* "You mentioned the kids and the dog are home a lot. Does it matter to you that the products we use around them are non-toxic and safe?"
* "Would it be more convenient to have a team of two or three in and out efficiently, rather than one person in your home most of the day?"
* "How important is it to you that everyone who enters your home is background checked, and that we're fully licensed, bonded, and insured?"
Then one short confirming line. Do not turn this into a monologue:
"That's exactly how we run every single job. Top to bottom, from the ceiling fans down to the baseboards, every visit."
▸  If it is a moving clean:
"And since you're moving, we also clean inside the appliances, drawers, and cabinets, so it's completely move-in ready."
4. Bridge their situation to the service
Connect their pain to the outcome. Fill the brackets with their own words from discovery.
"I know you've been [busy with work / caring for your mom / recovering]. Our whole goal is to give you your time back, so you can [rest / get back to the kids] instead of thinking about the cleaning. You said [favorite room] matters most to you. We're going to take that room top to bottom and make it the place you go to recharge. When we're done, I'm personally following up to make sure it got the white glove treatment."
That last line plants the review seed without asking them to promise anything before we have earned it.
Now, before you say a single number, get a yes on the outcome:
"If we could take all of that off your plate for you, would that make a real difference for you?"
Wait for the yes. Now your price competes against a result they just agreed they want, instead of against nothing.
5. Present pricing with the guarantee up front
"Here's your estimate. A one-time cleaning, which people usually do for a special occasion or the holidays, would be _____.
Most of our clients save money by going on a recurring schedule instead. There's no contract, you can cancel anytime, and it lets me build in savings for you.
The first visit is a little more, because it takes longer to bring the home up to our standard, and it's still less than that one-time price. That initial cleaning would be _____.
After that, most clients land on biweekly, because it keeps the home consistently clean without paying for weekly. That would be _____. We also do weekly or monthly if that fits you better.
Lead with one number and the recommendation. Give the weekly or monthly price only if they ask. A menu of four numbers just gets them comparison-shopping the cheapest option.
Now the guarantee, out loud, at the decision point. This is the close. You are not asking for the card here, you collect that in step 7.
"And here's why there's no risk to you: you don't pay a thing until the cleaning is done and you've walked through and told us it looks great. If anything's not right, we make it right before you pay a dollar. That's our guarantee."
"Which option sounds best for you?"
Assumptive close. You have named a recommendation (biweekly), so you are guiding, not listing. Stop talking and let them choose.
6. Schedule with urgency tied to their reason
Use their own deadline as the urgency. It is honest and it works.
"I can get your first cleaning in [before your guests arrive Friday / within a week]. Would a morning or an afternoon work better for you?"


Add scheduling info here
7. Confirm payment
"[First name], we keep payment simple. The way we hold your spot is a card on file, and like I said, we don't charge it until the job's done and you're happy. I can grab that number right now so I'm not bothering you later. Do you have it handy?"
Take it on the phone by default.
▸  If they are driving or cannot do it right now:
"No problem at all. I'll text you a secure link. If you can add it today, that locks in your appointment."
8. Close warm and low-key
"[First name], it's been good talking with you. We'll take care of your home on [day] between [time window], and we're looking forward to it. Reach out any time before then if anything comes up."
































WINDOW CLEANING
Residential: you quote and book this yourself. Commercial window cleaning: collect the info and schedule an estimate, do not quote it.
Know your three packages cold
GOLD (most complete, your price anchor): interior and exterior, screens removed and cleaned, window tracks cleaned, sills and frames detailed, PLUS a whole-house soft wash (low-pressure wash of the exterior).
SILVER (most popular, the one you recommend): interior and exterior, screens cleaned, sills and frames. No tracks, no soft wash.
BRONZE (budget, curb view): exterior windows and screens only. No interior.
Minimum job is $197. If the square-footage math lands below that, the price is $197, not the lower number.
Crew is one or two people. If they ask how long it takes, keep it vague: 'a few factors affect it, but plan for at least a few hours.' Never give a firm time, too short sounds overpriced and too long scares off the busy ones.
1. Verify the home
"I'm showing your home at roughly _____ square feet. Does that sound about right?"
Window pricing is based on square footage.
2. Discovery
"What made you decide to get the windows done right now?"
Now the consequence. Pick one:
* "When the afternoon light comes through, is that when the spots and haze bug you the most?"
* "How long have the windows been on your list that you keep meaning to get to?"
SWFL angle: hard water spots, salt air, love bug season, and pollen build up fast here, so there is usually real buildup to point to. If they mention selling or hosting, that is your urgency for step 6.
"Have you had them done before, or been tackling them yourself?"
▸  If they used a company before:
"What did you like, and what would you want done differently this time?"
▸  If they have been doing it themselves:
"How's that been going with the second-story and the exterior panes?"
DIY on exteriors and second-story is where people give up. Letting them say that out loud is what makes the quote feel worth it.
▸  If they only ask for the exterior (the Bronze scope):
Do not lock into exterior-only. Give them a reason to picture the inside glass, without pushing:
"Makes sense, the outside is what everyone sees from the street. When you're inside looking out, do the interior panes bug you too, or is it mainly the curb view you're after?"
If they admit the inside bugs them, you are now quoting Silver, not Bronze, and they widened it themselves. If they hold firm on exterior only, quote Bronze and earn the rest later.
3. Present the value as questions
"Let me put your quote together. While I do that, can I ask a couple of quick things so I get you the right option?"
* "Do you want just the glass cleaned, or the full detail with the screens, sills, and frames, so they actually look new and not just wiped down?"
* "How important is it to you that whoever is up on a ladder at your home is licensed, bonded, insured, and background checked?"
4. Bridge their situation to the service
"You said [the windows have been bugging you for months / you've got family coming / you're listing the house]. We'll get them clear enough that you notice the light again, and you won't have to think about them for a good while."
Then get a yes on the outcome before any number:
"If we could get all of that handled in one visit, would that take it off your plate?"
5. Present pricing with the guarantee up front
Anchor high with Gold, then land on Silver as your recommendation, then Bronze as the budget option. Remember the $197 minimum.
"We have three options, and I'll walk you through each so you can pick what fits.
The most complete is our Gold package. Full interior and exterior, we take the screens out and clean them, clean the window tracks, and detail the sills and frames. It also includes a whole-house soft wash, a low-pressure wash of the whole exterior, so the house looks fresh top to bottom. A lot of people add that while we're already out there. For your home that's _____.
Most people go with our Silver package, and it's the one I'd point you to. Same interior and exterior windows, screens cleaned, sills and frames done. It skips the tracks and the soft wash, which brings it down to _____.
And if you're really just after the curb view, our Bronze is exterior windows and screens only, no interior, at _____.
Here's why there's no risk either way: you don't pay until we're done and you've looked them over and you're happy. If we missed a spot, we make it right before you pay a dollar."
"Which one of those sounds most like what you're looking for?"
Silver is the popular one, so guide them there, but let them choose. If they ask you to re-explain a package, do it plainly, that is normal with three options.
6. Schedule
"I can get you on the schedule [before your event / this week]. Do mornings or afternoons work better for you?"
7. Confirm payment
"[First name], to hold your spot we take a card on file, and we don't charge it until the job's done and you're happy. Can I grab that number now so I'm not bothering you later?"
▸  If they are driving or cannot do it right now:
"No problem at all. I'll text you a secure link. If you can add it today, that locks in your appointment."
8. Close
"[First name], it's been good talking with you. We'll get your windows taken care of on [day], and we're looking forward to it."
9. After the job: earn the review
"So glad you're happy with how they turned out. Quick favor: we're a local business trying to serve more neighbors here in Southwest Florida. If you have two minutes, would you leave us a 5-star review on Google? It genuinely helps us grow."
▸  If this is a COMMERCIAL window cleaning job:
Do not quote it. Collect the property details and schedule an estimate.
"Happy to help with that. For a commercial property I'll get some details and set up a time for our owner to take a look, so we get you an accurate number."

AIRBNB / TURNOVER CLEANING
Do not quote this. Collect as much detail as you can, then Darius follows up with pricing today.
1. Set the expectation right away
"Happy to help with your turnover cleaning. Let me get some details about the property, and our owner will get back to you today with pricing."
Say 'today' only if you can keep it. Setting a same-day promise and hitting it is a big trust builder on turnover work, where owners are used to being ghosted.
2. Collect the property details
Get as much as you can. The more complete this is, the faster the quote goes out.
* Bedrooms  "How many bedrooms is the property?"
* Total beds  "And how many beds total, including any pull-out sofas or air mattresses?"
Beds drive the linen work, which is the real labor on a turnover. This is why we ask for beds and not just bedrooms.
* Bathrooms  "How many bathrooms?"
* Square footage  "About how many square feet is the place?"
* Urgency  "When do you need the first clean, and do you have guests booked already?"
If they have a checkout and check-in on the same day, that is a same-day turnover and it changes the scheduling. Flag it clearly in your notes.
3. Useful extras if the conversation allows
Not required, but each one saves a follow-up call.
* Do you supply the linens and towels, or would you want us to handle laundry on site?
* How often do you expect turnovers, roughly per month?
* Is there a lockbox or smart lock for access?
* Do you restock consumables (paper, soap, coffee), and would you want us doing that?
4. Close the call
"Perfect, I've got everything I need. Our owner will reach out today with pricing. He'll have your number and email, and if anything changes on your end just give us a call."
Log it and hand it to Darius the same day. Turnover leads go cold fast because they are usually shopping several cleaners at once.

PRESSURE WASHING
Do not quote this. Collect the details, then the owner follows up. Sometimes he can price it from what he finds online, sometimes he schedules a visit to see the property.
1. Set the expectation right away
"Happy to help with that. Let me get some details on the property, and our owner will follow up with an estimate."
Do not promise a price on the call. Some properties he can quote from satellite and street view, others need a walkthrough, and he decides which.
2. Collect the property details
* Square footage  "About how many square feet is the home?"
* Surfaces they asked for  "Which areas were you looking to have cleaned?"
* Urgency  "Is there a date you're working toward, or is this whenever we can fit it in?"
3. Ask about the other surfaces
Most people call about one surface and have not thought about the rest. Run the list, it is the single biggest way this job grows.
"While we're out there, are there any other areas you'd want us to take a look at? A lot of people have us do a few things at once since we're already on site."
* Driveway or walkways
* House soft wash (low-pressure wash of the whole exterior)
* Gutters
* Fencing
* Roof
Read them as options, not as an upsell pitch. 'Since we're already on site' is what makes it feel like convenience instead of a pitch.
4. Close the call
"Great, I've got what I need. Our owner will take a look and get back to you with an estimate. In some cases he can put a number together from what he can see online, and other times he'll set up a quick visit to walk the property. Either way you'll hear from him soon."
▸  If this is a COMMERCIAL pressure washing job:
Same path: collect the info and schedule an estimate. Do not quote it.
"For a commercial property I'll get some details and set up a time for our owner to take a look, so we get you an accurate number."

COMMERCIAL / HOA CLEANING
Do not quote this. Collect the details and book a walkthrough on Darius's calendar before the call ends.
1. Set the expectation right away
"Happy to help. For a commercial property the best next step is a quick walkthrough so we can put together an accurate proposal. Let me get a few details and then find a time that works for you."
Booking the walkthrough on the call is the whole goal here. A commercial lead that hangs up without a scheduled time usually goes quiet.
2. Collect the property details
* Business or association name  "What's the name of the business or association?"
* Property size  "About how large is the property, in square feet?"
* Frequency  "How often are you looking to have it cleaned? Daily, a few times a week, weekly?"
* Point of contact  "And who's the best person for us to work with on this?"
You already collected name, phone, and email at the start of the call. Confirm the contact is the same person, and if it is not, get the right one.
3. Useful extras if the conversation allows
* What type of property is it? Office, medical, retail, clubhouse, common areas?
* Are you currently with another cleaning company, or is this a new service?
* Is there a date you're hoping to have service started by?
If they are switching from another company, ask what is not working. That is the single most useful thing on the proposal.
4. Book the walkthrough
"Let's get our owner out to see the property. Would earlier in the week or later in the week work better for you?"
Offer a narrow choice, not an open calendar. Book it on Darius's calendar while they are still on the phone.
"Perfect, I've got you down for [day and time]. He'll walk the property with you and follow up with a proposal. You'll get a confirmation by email."
